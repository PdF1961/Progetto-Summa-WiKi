# Source: La sintesi tomistica

**Author**: Réginald Garrigou-Lagrange, O.P.
**Curatore edizione italiana**: Marco Bracchi; Presentazione di Mons. Antonio Livi
**First published**: as the article "Thomisme" in the *Dictionnaire de Théologie Catholique*; expanded as *La synthèse thomiste* (Desclée de Brouwer, 1950). Italian: Queriniana 1953 (tr. Ignazio Paci O.P.), fully revised edition Fede & Cultura 2015.
**Format**: epub, ~245,000 words (a full systematic treatise, comparable to a 700-page book)

## Scope and reading note

This is a far larger and more comprehensive work than [[essenza-e-attualita-del-tomismo]] — a complete systematic synthesis of Thomistic philosophy and theology in 8 Parts plus 4 appendices, originally written to be the "Thomisme" entry of a major theological dictionary. Given its size, this ingest began (2026-07-25, session 1) with a **verified full table of contents** plus deep reading of the Introduction, Part One, and Part Eight Chapter I, and was completed (2026-07-25, session 2) by reading **Parts Two through Seven in full** — covering De Deo Uno, the Trinity, angels and man, the Incarnation and Mariology, the Sacraments, and moral theology. Only Parte Ottava chapters II, III, and V (a small fraction of the whole) remain unread; see "Flagged for follow-up" below.

## Author's own stated plan (from the Prefazione)

> "Diremo anzitutto quali sono le principali opere in cui è esposta tale sintesi tomistica... Un'introduzione filosofica richiamerà alla mente la sintesi metafisica che la teologia tomistica presuppone. Esporremo poi quanto vi è di essenziale e di fondamentale in questa dottrina relativamente ai trattati *De Deo uno et trino*, *De Verbo Incarnato*, *De gratia*..."

## What Part One adds beyond [[essenza-e-attualita-del-tomismo]]

Same core doctrine (potency/act, essence/esse), but with a fuller derivation chain and new material:
- The chapter on **intelligible being and first principles**: being as the first object of the intellect (*ens est proprium obiectum intellectus*); the principle of contradiction/identity; the principle of **sufficient reason** (*ragion d'essere*) as analogically multivalent (intrinsic in essence/properties, efficient-causal, final, material); the **principle of substance**; the principle of **efficient causality** reformulated away from Humean "every phenomenon presupposes an antecedent phenomenon" toward "everything that comes into existence has an efficient cause"; the **principle of finality**.
- Moderate realism positioned between Platonic extreme realism and nominalism; connects to Descartes's *cogito* (the universal principle of non-contradiction is logically prior to "I think, therefore I am").
- A direct rebuttal of the Suarezian **Fr. Descoqs S.J.** objection that the First Thomistic Thesis misrepresents Aquinas — Garrigou-Lagrange answers with a chain of direct Aquinas citations (*Quodlibet III*, Commento alle Sentenze, *Contra Gentiles*) showing real (not merely logical) composition of essence/esse in every creature.
- The **potenza obbedienziale** (obediential potency) distinction: for Aquinas it is a *passive* potency (mere non-repugnance to elevation); for Suárez it is *active*, which Garrigou-Lagrange says would make it simultaneously natural and supernatural — a confusion of orders.
- Extension into the supernatural order: in Christ there is only **one act of existence** for the two natures (communicated by the Word); Suárez, denying the real essence/esse distinction, is committed to two existences in Christ, weakening the hypostatic union.

## What Part Two adds: the full De Deo Uno treatise

Read in full — the theological-epistemological and metaphysical core of Thomistic natural theology, given its own concept pages: [[Le Cinque Vie]] (Cap. II) and [[Grazia Efficace e Premozione Fisica]] (Capp. IV-VII); the "eminence of the Deity" / analogy-of-divine-attributes material (Cap. III) folded into [[Gerarchia degli Esseri e Analogia]]. Highlights not captured elsewhere:

- **Cap. I, on the nature of theology itself**: a careful epistemology of theological reasoning, positioning Garrigou-Lagrange's own view between two rejected extremes (P. Marin-Sola, who holds strictly illative theological conclusions can be defined as dogma of faith, vs. P. Charlier, who holds theology can never reach certain conclusions of its own, only borrow metaphysics's). Distinguishes **eight distinct modes** by which theology works with revealed truth: (1) positive collection of revealed truths from Scripture/Tradition/Magisterium; (2) conceptual analysis of a revealed truth's exact subject/predicate (e.g. what "*Verbum caro factum est*" means); (3) defense against objections (showing non-contradiction, not positive intrinsic possibility — proving a mystery's possibility would also prove its existence, since revealed mysteries are necessary not contingent); (4) arguments of fittingness (*convenientia*) that manifest without demonstrating a mystery's truth; (5) *explicative* discourse drawing out what is already formally contained in a revealed truth (e.g. "the Word, consubstantial with the Father, was made man" as the *explicit* form of "the Word was made flesh" — not a new inferred truth); (6) strictly illative discourse deducing a **third truth also independently revealed** elsewhere in Scripture/Tradition (e.g., from "Jesus is truly God" + "Jesus is truly man" to "Christ has two intellects and two free wills," confirmed by Christ's own words in Gethsemane) — such conclusions **can** be defined as dogma; (7) illative discourse deducing a third truth **revealed only in the other two**, not independently elsewhere — Thomists generally hold these too can be defined as dogma, since reasoning here only relates two sufficient premises rather than causing new knowledge; (8) illative discourse from **one revealed premise + one merely rational premise**, yielding a conclusion revealed only *virtualiter* (in its cause), not *simpliciter* — the true "theological conclusion" properly so called, which **cannot** be defined as dogma (though its contradictory can be condemned as "erroneous," a lesser censure than "heretical"). Garrigou-Lagrange credits Aquinas's own First Question of the *Summa* with using all eight modes, and connects dogmatic *progress* to the conceptual-analysis mode (2) — moving from confused common-sense/scriptural notions to distinct technical ones (essence/existence, nature/person/relation for Trinity and Incarnation, habitual/actual and efficacious/sufficient grace, etc.) — the historically-sensitive process he compares to Newman-style doctrinal development, while still rejecting strong "evolution of dogma" claims.
- **Cap. II, the five ways**: see [[Le Cinque Vie]] for full detail — includes the technical "proper cause" (*causa propria*) argument distinguishing the causal chain the proofs require (essentially/necessarily subordinated, presently existing) from the causal chain they do *not* need (accidentally subordinated past generations), and a direct rebuttal of Kant's objection that the principle of causality's necessity might be merely subjective.
- **Cap. III, eminence of the Deity**: see [[Gerarchia degli Esseri e Analogia]] for the *formaliter eminenter* doctrine and Cajetan's solution to how divine perfections identify without destroying each other. Key corollary: **sanctifying grace is a physical, formal, analogical participation in the Deity as it is in itself** (not merely in God-as-known, i.e. *ipsum esse subsistens*) — this is why grace, uniquely among all creaturely likenesses to God (minerals resembling God in being, plants in life, angels/humans in intelligence by nature), resembles God *precisely as God*, and is the "radical principle" of the beatific vision.
- **Cap. IV, God's knowledge**: God's knowledge is not discursive but a single intuition of everything he can and does produce; the science of "simple intelligence" (of possibles) vs. "of vision/approbation" (of what he wills/permits); introduces futuribles as the basis for the middle-knowledge controversy resolved in Capp. V-VI.
- **Capp. V-VII**: see [[Grazia Efficace e Premozione Fisica]] for the full treatment of antecedent/consequent will, sufficient/efficacious grace, physical premotion vs. simultaneous concourse, the principle of predilection, predestination, creation *ex nihilo* and its freedom (against Leibniz's "best of all possible worlds" necessitarianism — "*dum Deus calculat, fit mundus*" is rebutted at length), conservation, and divine motion.

## What Part Three adds: the full Trinity treatise

Read in full — see the dedicated concept page [[La Santissima Trinità (Sintesi Tomistica)]] for complete detail on: the Greek-Fathers-to-Augustine-to-Aquinas historical arc; the two processions (generation via intellect, spiration via love); divine relations and the "*unum esse*" dispute with Suárez (the exact same essence/esse fault line as the Christological dispute); Persons as subsistent relations; the notional-acts circularity objection and its resolution; equality and mutual indwelling of the Persons; the Trinity's unknowability by natural reason; proper names vs. appropriations; and the three rival accounts (Vázquez, Suárez, Aquinas) of the Trinity's indwelling in the just soul.

## What Part Four adds: angels and man

Read in full — see the dedicated concept page [[Angeli e Anima Umana (Sintesi Tomistica)]] for full detail on: angelic nature (pure spirits, no two of the same species), angelic knowledge (intuitive, innate, universal-yet-concrete ideas) and will (necessary love of the connatural good, irrevocable choice), the state of angels before/after their trial and their relation to Christ's merits; the treatise on man (soul as sole substantial form, against Averroism and pre-Thomist plurality-of-forms), spirituality/immortality of the soul (three degrees of abstraction, the natural-desire argument), the real distinction of faculties and the Thomist definition of freedom against Molina, the separated soul's individuation and immutable will, and the historical development of Aquinas's own doctrine that original justice included sanctifying grace.

## What Part Five adds: Incarnation, hypostatic union, and Mariology

Read in full — see the dedicated concept page [[Incarnazione, Unione Ipostatica e Mariologia]], which also resolves the "ontological personality" question flagged as unread in [[Persona (Tomismo)]]. Covers: the motive of the Incarnation (Thomist "if Adam had not sinned" vs. Scotus/Suárez); ontological personality and Cajetan's formula; *unum esse in Christo* (paralleling the Trinity's *unum esse* — same Suárez fault line); the intrinsically infinite value of Christ's merits; freedom and impeccability reconciled; the Passion's five modes of causality; and Mariology (predestination, divine maternity's dignity, the documented development in Aquinas's own Immaculate Conception texts, fullness of grace, universal mediation).

## What Part Six adds: the Sacraments

Read in full — see [[I Sacramenti nella Sintesi Tomistica]] for full detail: sacraments as physical instrumental causes of grace; transubstantiation (against Scotus's "adductive" theory and Suárez); the sacrifice of the Mass (sacramental, not physical, immolation — Aquinas anticipating and Trent answering the Reformers' objection); attrition vs. contrition (the "initial love of benevolence" doctrine); the revival of merits controversy with Suárez; and the immutability of the separated soul after death.

## What Part Seven adds: moral theology in full

Read in full — see [[Teologia Morale nella Sintesi Tomistica]] for complete detail: last end and beatitude (with a proof of God's existence from natural desire); the psychology and morality of human acts, including the probabilism controversy (Medina, Alexander VII, Gardeil/Beaudouin's equiprobabilist reconciliation); habitus, the acquired/infused virtue distinction, sin's analogical mortal/venial division; law (natural law's immutability against Scotus and Ockham); the full treatise on grace (necessity, essence as physical participation in the Deity, divisions, sufficient/efficacious grace, obediential potency, justification, the four analogical senses of merit); the theological virtues' formal motives (faith's *veritas prima*, hope's *Deus auxilians* against the Quietists, charity as supernatural friendship); the cardinal virtues; Christian perfection (love superior to knowledge *in via*) and the three kinds of life; and the charisms, especially Aquinas's doctrine of biblical inspiration via principal/instrumental causality.

This completes the systematic reading of **all eight Parts** of *La Sintesi Tomistica* except Parte Ottava's chapters II, III, and V (realism of first principles vs. pragmatism, and the efficacious/sufficient grace chapter — this last topic is now however extensively covered via Parte Settima above).

## What Part Eight Ch. I adds: the origin and stakes of the XXIV Tesi

Corrects/extends [[XXIV Tesi Tomiste]]: the theses were **approved by Pius X's motu proprio of 29 June 1914** (not 1925 — 1925 is when Garrigou-Lagrange presented their applications at the International Thomistic Congress in Rome, the material later published as Part II of [[essenza-e-attualita-del-tomismo]]). Historical chain: Card. Villeneuve's diagnosis of pre-1914 doctrinal chaos (everyone claiming Aquinas for mutually contradictory positions) → Pius X's motu proprio requiring *principia et pronuntiata maiora doctrinae Sancti Thomae* be taught → Sacred Congregation of Studies commissions 24 theses from Thomist professors → February 1916 decree makes the *Summa* the set text and the theses "*tutae normae directivae*" (safe directive norms, not de fide) → Benedict XV confirms (7 March 1916) → 1917 Code of Canon Law canon 1366 §2 codifies "*ad Angelici Doctoris rationem, doctrinam et principia*" → Guido Mattiussi S.J. publishes the standard commentary (1917).

Garrigou-Lagrange's polemical point: some historians treat the potency/act distinction as a mere "hypothesis" or fruitful postulate rather than a demonstrated necessary truth — if so, every Thomistic proof of God's existence built on it collapses to probabilism. He reprises, in condensed form, the same being/essence/existence/causality chain of applications as Part One and [[Dottrina di Atto e Potenza]], citing Fr. Pierre Charles S.J. on how loss of metaphysical grounding produced modernism (psychology replacing ontology, subjectivism replacing revelation, history replacing dogma).
