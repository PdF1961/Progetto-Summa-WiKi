# Source: Summa contro i Gentili — structural entry

**Author**: San Tommaso d'Aquino
**This edition**: Italian translation by P. Tito S. Centi O.P. (the same translator behind [[somma-teologica-struttura]]'s Italian edition), UTET (Unione Tipografico-Editrice Torinese), 1st ed. 1975.
**Format**: OCR'd PDF, 1,374 pages, 41.3MB

## Why this is a structural entry, not a full ingest

At 1,374 pages this is a complete major *summa* — comparable in scale to [[la-sintesi-tomistica]] or [[sul-male-de-malo]] but larger than either. This entry reads and records Centi's substantial scholarly **Introduzione** (pp. 9-14+ sampled directly) in depth, and records the work's standard four-book structure from established Thomistic scholarship (cross-verified against [[le-thomisme-gilson]]'s bibliography, which dates the work 1258-1264/65 depending on Mandonnet vs. Grabmann's chronology) rather than attempting to read the body text.

## The Introduction's historical debates (read directly, pp. 9-14)

**On the title**: not original to Aquinas. The oldest attested and probably intended title is *Liber de Veritate Catholicae Fidei contra errores infidelium* — chosen because Aquinas held that the "sin of infidelity" belongs not only to those who never accepted the Christian faith but to anyone who departs from it, including heretics (*S.Th.* II-II, q.10). So the *infideles* targeted are collectively: gentiles/pagans, Muslims, Jews, heretics, Greek schismatics, and condemned false teachers. "Liber de Veritate" was avoided as a title because it duplicated the name of the *Quaestiones disputatae de veritate*; "Summa" (compendium) plus "gentiles" (used loosely as a near-synonym for the fuller "infideles") produced the familiar shorthand, attested in Paris booksellers' lists by c. 1277.

**On the work's purpose and occasion** — a genuine scholarly controversy, presented with both sides: 
- The traditional account (a 14th-century Catalan chronicler, Peter Marsili, cited at length in the original Latin) holds St. Raymond of Peñafort (former Master General of the Dominicans) commissioned the work to aid the conversion of Muslims and Jews in post-*Reconquista* Spain and evangelization in Africa/Asia — making it, at least in intent, an apologetic/missionary manual.
- **Fr. R.-A. Gauthier's** revisionist philological research (cited and largely credited by Centi) discredits several popular legends: that Aquinas wrote the work on odd scraps of paper; Mandonnet's theory that Aquinas released the text serially before finishing it; and the famous legend of Aquinas pounding the table at King Louis IX's dinner table, which Gauthier shows has no chronological connection to the *Contra Gentiles* despite P. Marc's defense of it. Gauthier's strongest objection to the Peñafort commission story: Aquinas's usual practice was to dedicate commissioned works to their requester, and he did not do so here.
- Centi's own mediating position: the request may have been made *impersonally* — collectively, to the Dominican masters of Paris (Aquinas among them) at the 1259 Valenciennes general chapter, which also founded the missionary Arabic-language study center Peñafort had promoted — which would explain both Peter Marsili's testimony and the absence of a personal dedication. Gauthier's own dating has Aquinas beginning the work in 1259, completing 45 chapters before returning to Italy.
- **Explicit clarification, load-bearing for how this vault should classify the work**: whatever its occasion, the *Contra Gentiles* is a **formally theological work**, not a "Summa philosophica" as some have relabeled it — despite containing extensive, profound metaphysical speculation, that speculation always serves systematic theology's task of grounding the doctrinal synthesis of Christian thought.
- Cites Dom Pietro Marc's monumental 683-page introductory study (Marietti, 1967) as the fullest but critically unreliable repository of prior scholarship — Centi explicitly judges Marc (and collaborator P. Ceslao Pera) to lack critical judgment proportionate to their diligence, several of their conclusions "risking wholesale rejection despite their erudition."

## Structure (standard four books, per established Thomistic scholarship — not independently re-verified page-by-page in this pass)

- **Libro I** — God's existence and nature knowable by natural reason: existence of God, divine attributes (simplicity, perfection, goodness, infinity, immutability, eternity, unity, God's own self-knowledge, will).
- **Libro II** — Creation: procession of creatures from God, the created world's order, the angels, the human soul (its origin, union with body, immortality — the treatise most directly cross-referenced by [[god-philosophy-universities]] ch. 9's discussion of soul/body identity).
- **Libro III** — Divine providence and governance of creatures; the last end of rational creatures (beatitude); evil and its relation to providence (cross-references [[Il Male come Privazione]] and [[sul-male-de-malo]]); human free will and law.
- **Libro IV** — Truths knowable only by revelation: the Trinity, the Incarnation, the sacraments, the resurrection of the body, and the last things — the natural continuation, in a more purely philosophical mode, of what [[la-sintesi-tomistica]] Parti Terza-Sesta cover for the *Summa Theologiae*.

## Significance for this vault

The *Contra Gentiles* is the primary companion text to the *Summa Theologiae* ([[somma-teologica-struttura]]) — same author, overlapping period (per Gilson's bibliography, largely written 1259-1264, i.e. before the *Summa*'s Prima Pars), but organized as sustained philosophical argument for a target audience assumed not to share Christian premises, rather than as a *quaestio*-by-*quaestio* theological synthesis. Centi's introduction, read here, gives this vault its first direct look at the kind of source-critical, philological Thomistic scholarship (Chenu, Gauthier, Mandonnet, Grabmann, Marc) that underlies the more synthetic secondary sources already ingested ([[la-sintesi-tomistica]], [[le-thomisme-gilson]]).
