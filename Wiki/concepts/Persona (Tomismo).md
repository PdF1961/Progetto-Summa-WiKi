# Persona (Person)

Per [[mondin-dizionario-tommaso]]. A historically Christian philosophical concept, absent from classical pagan thought, which recognized no absolute value in the individual as such.

## Origin: a Christian invention

Neither Greek nor Latin had a word for "person" in this sense before Christianity. The concept emerged from Trinitarian and Christological disputes: **Augustine** (*De Trinitate*) needed a term applicable distinctly to Father, Son, and Spirit without collapsing into tritheism or dissolving their individuality — "essence"/"substance" name what the three share, while Greek *hypostasis* / Latin *persona* name what is singular. Applied analogically to man too: "*singulus quisque homo... una persona est*."

## Boethius's definition

*"Persona est rationalis naturae individua substantia"* — an individual substance of rational nature (*Contra Eutychen et Nestorium*, c.4). All four elements are load-bearing: individuality alone belongs even to accidents; substance/nature alone can be merely generic (a rock or a cat is an individual substance without being a person); adding **rationality** as specific difference is what yields personhood.

## Aquinas's assessment and ontological elevation

Adopts Boethius's definition over rivals (Richard of St. Victor's; the "masters' definition" after Alan of Lille), refining the sense of "substantia" as *prima substantia* (singular subject/*suppositum*) rather than *secunda substantia* (universal essence) — a person is always a singular subsistent, never a universal.

Aquinas gives personhood an extremely high ontological status: **"Persona significat id quod est perfectissimum in tota natura"** (*I, q.28, a.3*) — the most perfect thing in the whole of nature. Viewed from [[Dottrina di Atto e Potenza]]: person is the mode in which *esse* (*actualitas omnium actuum*) reaches its fullest, most complete actuation. Hence God, angels, and man — everything entitled "person" — possess infinite dignity and absolute value.

## Analogical predication

"Person" is said **analogically**, not univocally, across God, angels, and man (*secundum prius et posterius*), always designating the same fundamental perfection: individual subsistence in the order of spirit. *"Omne subsistens in natura rationali vel intellectuali est persona"* (*C.G.* IV, c.35).

## Historical-civilizational significance

The uniqueness and irrepeatable singularity of the person, and the consequent substantial equality in dignity of every human being regardless of class, wealth, or race, is presented as a specifically Christian truth of civilizational consequence — it transformed pagan culture, which measured worth by social rank, into the *respublica christiana* of the Middle Ages.

## Ontological personality resolved (Christology and Trinity)

[[la-sintesi-tomistica]] Parte Quinta, Cap. II gives Garrigou-Lagrange's full treatment of "ontological personality" (the question flagged here as unread is now resolved) — see [[Incarnazione, Unione Ipostatica e Mariologia]] for complete detail. Cajetan's formula, endorsed by the majority of Thomists: personality is *id quo natura singularis fit immediate capax existentiae* — that by which a singular nature becomes immediately capable of receiving existence. Applied to the Trinity ([[La Santissima Trinità (Sintesi Tomistica)]]), this is why the three divine Persons (three personalities) share only **one** act of existence; applied to Christ, this is why the hypostatic union entails **one** act of existence for two natures (*unum esse in Christo*) — the same essence/esse framework documented in [[Dottrina di Atto e Potenza]].
