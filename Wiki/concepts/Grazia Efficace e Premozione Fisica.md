# Grazia Efficace e Premozione Fisica

The classical Thomist (Bañezian) account of divine causality, providence, and grace — per [[la-sintesi-tomistica]] Parte Seconda, Capp. IV-VII. The single most technically contested doctrine in the Thomist/Molinist controversy (*De Auxiliis*).

## God's knowledge of futures: against "middle knowledge"

God's knowledge does not depend on or get measured by created things (that would introduce passivity into Pure Act). God knows all possibles (*scientia simplicis intelligentiae*, prior to any decree) and all actual/future things via his own decree of will (*scientia visionis/approbationis*). **Futuribles** (conditioned futures — what a free creature *would* do under circumstances that never obtain) are known via a *conditional* decree of the will, and are a *tertium quid* between the merely possible and the actual.

Thomists reject **Molina's "scientia media"** (middle knowledge: God's knowledge of conditioned free futures *prior to and independent of* any divine decree) as introducing dependency/passivity into God relative to a creaturely determination not caused by him. The Thomist dilemma pressed against Molinism: **"if God is not determining, he is determined"** — there is no third option. God's knowledge of future contingents is not knowledge of them *as future* but as eternally *present* in the single unmoving instant of eternity (*aeternitas ambit totum tempus* — like a mountain-top observer seeing an entire army file through a valley in one glance) — but an event is only "present in eternity" because God has willed or permitted it.

## Antecedent vs. consequent will; sufficient vs. efficacious grace

God's will is **antecedent** (conditional, inefficacious — willing a good *in itself*, e.g. the salvation of all, "if other things being equal") or **consequent** (absolute, efficacious — willing a good *hic et nunc*, actually accomplished). This is, for the Thomist school, the foundation of the distinction between:
- **Sufficient grace** (from the antecedent/conditional will) — gives the *real power* to observe the precepts.
- **Efficacious grace** (from the consequent/absolute will) — gives *actual, free* observance *hic et nunc*.

"There is more in the act than in the mere power to act, as there is more in the act of seeing than in the faculty of sight." Resisting sufficient grace is a purely creaturely defect; *not* resisting it is a good that ultimately comes from God. Efficacious grace is efficacious **intrinsically** (Thomist view — it is efficacious because God so willed it and moves the will to it) rather than **extrinsically** (Molinist view — efficacious only because of a foreseen free consent, known via *scientia media*).

## Physical premotion (praemotio physica)

The Thomist account of *how* God's efficacious will operates without destroying freedom. Against **Molina and Suárez's "simultaneous concourse"** (God's concourse acts directly on the *effect* jointly with the creature, "as when two men pull a boat" — meaning the second cause is *coordinated* with, not *subordinated* to, the first cause), Thomists hold God **premoves** and **applies** every second cause to its act (*applicat eas ad agendum*), since operation follows being, and only Pure Act (God) acts entirely of itself — every participated being acts only in dependence on divine motion. Molina's own objection (quoted directly from the *Concordia*): "I do not see what that motion and application is by which God moves and applies second causes to act." The Thomist reply: without premotion, the second cause would pass from potency to act by itself, meaning the more perfect would come from the less perfect — undermining the very principle of causality the proofs of God's existence (the first two ways) rest on.

**Key Thomist qualification (repeatedly defended against a standing objection)**: this predetermination is **non-necessitating**. God moves the will "immutably, because of the efficacy of the moving power, which cannot fail; but because of the nature of the will moved, which is indifferently disposed toward various things, no necessity is introduced, but freedom remains" (citing *De malo*). God moves each cause *according to its own nature* — moving free causes freely, natural causes naturally — "as an artist plays the harp, the violin, and other stringed instruments each differently." Examples given: the Virgin Mary's free *fiat* at the Annunciation, and Paul's conversion, were both efficaciously, infallibly willed by God from eternity *and* freely performed — "a virginal contact between efficacious grace and freedom, which does not violate but enriches."

## The "principle of predilection" and predestination

Answering *why* God predestines one person rather than another: **"since God's love is the cause of the goodness of things... nothing would be better than another thing, if God did not will a greater good for one than for another"** (*Summa* citation) — i.e., no one is better than another except by being more loved and more helped by God. Hence **love precedes election, and election precedes predestination** — "all the predestined are elected and loved" — and future merits are the *effect*, never the *cause*, of predestination (against semi-Pelagianism). God is never a mere spectator of the good in the human will; even "the beginning of good will" must come from God, source of all good.

Predestination's rationale: in the predestined, God wills to manifest his goodness as **mercy** (raising them from their faults); in the reprobate (whose final impenitence he only *permits*, never *positively wills*), he manifests his **justice**. Why God elects this one rather than that one — Augustine: "*noli velle iudicare, si non vis errare*" ("do not seek to judge, lest you err") — there is, beyond the principle of predilection, no further reason than the "divine good pleasure" (*beneplacitum*).

## The balancing principles against all deviations

Two principles must be held together to avoid, on one side, predestinarianism/Protestant determinism/Jansenism, and on the other, Pelagianism/semi-Pelagianism:
1. **God never commands the impossible** — sufficient grace really is given to all, making observance genuinely possible.
2. **No one is better than another except by being more loved and helped by God** ("*quid habes quod non accepisti?*").

The "insondabile mistero" these two principles bound — the reconciliation of universal salvific will with a gratuitous, particular predestination — reduces, per Garrigou-Lagrange, to the same "eminence of the Deity" documented in [[la-sintesi-tomistica]]'s Cap. III (see [[Dottrina di Atto e Potenza]]): infinite mercy, infinite justice, and sovereign liberty identify without destroying each other in the Deity, which remains hidden from us short of the beatific vision.

## Divine conservation and motion (creation's continuation)

God's causality doesn't stop at creation *ex nihilo* (which only God can perform, since the effect — being itself — is the most universal effect, requiring the most universal cause) and its temporal (not eternal) beginning (an article of faith, not demonstrable — the world's beginning depends solely on free divine choice, not metaphysical necessity, so its eternity or temporality can't be settled by reason alone). It continues in **conservation** (creatures, being composed of essence/participated existence, would be instantly annihilated if God ceased conserving them, "as light ceases when the sun disappears") and in **motion** (God applies every second cause to its operation) — against both **occasionalism** (only God truly acts; fire doesn't really heat) and **Suárez/Molina's simultaneous concourse** (second cause coordinated with, not subordinated to, God).
