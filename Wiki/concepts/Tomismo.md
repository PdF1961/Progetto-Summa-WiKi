# Tomismo

The philosophical-theological system of St. Thomas Aquinas as a living school, distinct from a merely historical reading of his texts.

## Defining characters (per Garrigou-Lagrange, [[essenza-e-attualita-del-tomismo]])

1. **Realism** — primacy of being over knowledge; knowledge is relative to being, not the reverse. Intellectual knowledge begins from the idea of being and completes itself in judgment, whose "soul" is the verb *to be*.
2. **Intellectualism** — the primacy of intelligence (the faculty of being) over will, valid for both divine and human intellect. Opposed to voluntarism (*stat pro ratione voluntas*).
3. **Theocentrism** — the primacy of God, Pure Act, over all creation, since act is more perfect than potency. Nothing exists or perseveres in existence except through God as creator and conserver; no creature acts without God's concurrence.

From these three flow secondary characters: organic unity (not eclectic or artificially forced), universality, elevation and depth of principles, precision of terminology (avoids metaphor except when necessary), and harmony among the parts (sense/intellect, tradition/personal effort, intelligence/freedom, reason/faith).

## Why it is held to remain "attuale" (contemporary)

Garrigou-Lagrange's central polemical claim: theology that abandons Thomism's immutable metaphysical notions (in favor of Blondel's philosophy of action, or a "conformity of mind with ever-evolving human life") collapses into modernism, relativism, and ultimately cannot ground the immutability of dogma or the demonstrative force of the proofs of God's existence. See [[Dottrina di Atto e Potenza]] for the metaphysical core this rests on.

## Contrast with rival scholastic positions

- **Suárez**: sought a middle way between Aquinas and Scotus; tends to treat potency as a kind of imperfect act, and causes in a subordinated series as coordinated rather than truly subordinated to a first cause. Garrigou-Lagrange treats this as a weakening that reopens Parmenides's/Spinoza's argument against the plurality of beings.
- **Scotus**: univocity of being (*ens* said univocally of God and creatures) — opposed to Aquinas's analogy of being.

## Gilson's alternative synthesis: being, hierarchy, analogy

Étienne Gilson's [[le-thomisme-gilson]] offers a differently-centered synthesis of the same system: rather than starting from act/potency as Garrigou-Lagrange does, Gilson derives everything from the convertibility of being/unity/truth/goodness and the analogical, hierarchical structure of participated being — explicitly arguing this hierarchy is *not* Neo-Platonic emanationism but "an ordered discontinuity" grounded in Augustine's personal Creator-God. See [[Gerarchia degli Esseri e Analogia]] and [[Étienne Gilson]] for the historical framing of Aquinas's task as "christianizing Aristotle" between Latin Averroism and Augustinian-Franciscan Platonism.

See [[Il Cardine della Metafisica Tomista]] for a direct comparison of Garrigou-Lagrange's, Gilson's, and Mondin's candidate "keystones" for the whole system.
