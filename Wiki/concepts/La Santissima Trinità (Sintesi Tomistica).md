# La Santissima Trinità

Aquinas's Trinitarian theology per [[la-sintesi-tomistica]] Parte Terza (9 chapters, read in full).

## From the Greek Fathers to Augustine to Aquinas

The **Greek Fathers** (against Sabellius's denial of real distinction of Persons, and Arius/Macedonius's denial of the Son's/Spirit's divinity) started from the revealed Trinity of Persons and worked to reconcile it with unity of nature via **consubstantiality** (defined at Nicaea) — but left obscure *why* there are exactly two processions, why only the first is "generation," and why the Father alone is called "Creator" in the Creed despite creative power belonging to all three Persons equally.

**Augustine** (*De Trinitate*) reverses the direction: starting from the philosophically-demonstrated unity of nature ([[la-sintesi-tomistica]] Parte Seconda / *De Deo Uno*) and ascending to the Trinity of Persons via revelation. His key insight: the Son proceeds from the Father as **Word** (*Verbum*, John 1:1-3) — an interior intellectual "word," not accidental (as our own mental word is) but **substantial**, since in God thinking is not an accident but the divine substance itself. This clarifies why there are exactly **two processions**: intellect and love are the two forms of spiritual activity, so if the Son proceeds as Word (intellectual procession), the Holy Spirit must proceed as the term of a procession of **love** — the foundation of the Augustinian relations (Father *ad Filium*, Son *ad Patrem*, Spirit *ad utrumque*). Augustine still leaves obscure exactly why only the first procession is "generation."

**Aquinas** completes Augustine's account and resolves its residual difficulties, notably: distinguishing *essential* intellection/love (common to all three Persons) from the Father's proper "utterance" of the Word and from *notional* love (spiration) vs. *personal* love (the Holy Spirit himself) — otherwise the objection "all three Persons understand/love, so all three should generate/spirate, ad infinitum" would stand.

## The two processions

1. **Generation of the Son**: an intellectual procession (*emanatio intelligibilis*) — neither an effect-to-cause relation (Arianism) nor a mere being-of-reason (Modalism), but interior and real, of the *same nature* as the Father. Principle: "what proceeds interiorly by intellectual process need not be different — indeed, the more perfectly it proceeds, the more it is identified with its source." Since God's intellection is *subsistent* (not accidental, as ours is), the Word it produces must be **substantial** — true God from true God — which is why this procession properly merits the name **generation** (a living being proceeding from a living being, receiving a similar nature).
2. **Spiration of the Holy Spirit**: a procession by way of **love**, as in us love of the good proceeds from knowledge of the good ("*amor meus, pondus meum*," Augustine). Not a generation, because love, unlike knowledge, doesn't assimilate its object to itself but tends toward it.

## Divine relations and "unum esse"

Real processions ground **real relations**: paternity/filiation (from generation), active spiration/passive spiration-or-procession (from the love-procession) — four relations total, though active spiration doesn't really distinguish from paternity/filiation (it opposes only passive spiration). These relations are **not really distinct from the divine essence** (God has no accidents) — their *esse in* (inherence) is substantial, identical with God's *esse* (existence) itself, hence **"tres Personae non habent nisi unum esse"** (the three Persons have only one act of existing). **Suárez, consistent with his denial of the real essence/existence distinction in creatures, denies this too** — for him the divine relations can only be real through their own proper existence, committing him to *three relative existences in God*, which then cannot resolve the classic objection "things identical to a third thing are identical to each other" (since the divine Persons are each really identical to the one divine essence) without ad hoc exceptions. This is the *exact same fault line*, per Garrigou-Lagrange, as the Christological dispute over "*unum esse in Christo*" documented in [[Dottrina di Atto e Potenza]] — both trace to Suárez's rejection of the real essence/esse distinction.

Relations *opposed* to each other (paternity vs. filiation, active vs. passive spiration) **are** really distinct from each other, even though each is really identical to the one divine essence — resolved via the geometrical analogy: the three angles of a triangle are really distinct from each other (by relational opposition) while none opposes the surface area common to all three.

## Persons as subsistent relations

A person in general: an intelligent, free, individual subject of rational nature (Boethius's *rationalis naturae individua substantia* — see [[Persona (Tomismo)]]). Since in God everything is one and the same except where relational opposition obtains, what distinguishes the three Persons can only be the three opposed relations — and since these relations are **subsistent** (not accidents) and **incommunicable** (mutually opposed), they possess exactly the two marks of personhood (subsistence + incommunicability). Hence: **a divine Person is a subsistent relation** (*relatio ut subsistens*; "*persona est quam distinctum relatione subsistens in essentia divina*," *De potentia*). Cajetan's gloss: divine reality in itself is neither purely absolute (nature) nor purely relative (Person) but eminently contains both *formaliter eminenter* — echoing the same doctrine documented for the divine attributes generally in [[Gerarchia degli Esseri e Analogia]].

## Notional acts and the circularity objection

The hardest objection ever raised against the Augustinian account: paternity (as relation) is founded on active generation, so it can't conceptually precede it — but the Father's *personhood* must precede his act of generation, or there's vicious circularity if personhood = the relation of paternity. **Aquinas's resolution**: paternity considered *as relation* does presuppose the notional act; but considered *as constitutive of the person*, the notional act presupposes the relation instead — no contradiction, because paternity isn't considered under the same aspect in both directions (parallel to how, in the single indivisible instant of ensoulment, the body's ultimate disposition to receive the soul precedes the soul in the order of material causality but follows it in the order of formal/efficient/final causality). Notional acts are **necessary, not free** (though spontaneously willed — the Father wants to generate the Son as he wants to be God), and belong to the divine nature *as it exists in the Father* (generation) or *in the Father and Son together* (spiration) — not to the nature as such (else all three Persons would generate/spirate, infinitely).

## Equality and mutual indwelling ("perichoresis")

Because the three Persons share numerically one nature/existence (*unum esse*), they are strictly equal (like the three angles of an equilateral triangle) — generating is not more perfect than being generated, since generation only *communicates*, not *causes*, the Father's nature. The Persons are the "eminent exemplar of the life of charity": "*et mea omnia tua sunt et tua mea sunt*" (John 17:10) — the union of charity that should exist among souls is meant to reflect the union among the divine Persons (John 17:21).

## Unknowability by natural reason

The Trinity cannot be known naturally: natural reason can know God only as principle of things via creative power, which is common to all three Persons and reveals only the divine *essence*, not the plurality of Persons. Nor can reason positively demonstrate the Trinity's intrinsic possibility (only its non-repugnance) — because, since the Trinity is a *necessary* not contingent truth, demonstrating its real possibility would also demonstrate its existence (Vatican Council: mysteries properly so called "cannot be understood and demonstrated from natural principles").

## Divine indwelling in the just soul

Three rival accounts, per Aquinas/John of St. Thomas/Fr. Gardeil: **Vázquez** reduces all divine presence in the just to the general presence of immensity (God present in all conserved things) — the Trinity as merely a beloved-but-absent object. **Suárez** holds charity alone makes God substantially present, regardless of the presence of immensity — but Garrigou-Lagrange objects that charity for Christ's humanity or the Virgin doesn't make *them* really present within us; charity is affective union, not real indwelling. **Aquinas's own view** (which "dominates" both): the special Trinitarian presence in the just *presupposes* the general presence of immensity (against Suárez), but sanctifying grace, infused virtues, and gifts render God really present *in a new way*, as an object experientially knowable and enjoyable, "quasi-experimental" via infused charity's connaturality with God's inner life (against Vázquez) — not merely represented as an absent beloved, but really present and sometimes *felt*, as the "soul of our soul." This experiential-but-often-latent knowledge need not be actual (the Trinity indwells even during sleep, as long as one remains in grace) — only really possible via the habitual gifts.

## Verified against the primary text (2026-07-26)

*S.Th.* I, qq.27, 29, 40 — read directly, see [[somma-teologica-trinita]] — confirm the processions/relations/persons ordering, Boethius's person-definition as adopted verbatim at q.29 a.1, and the "relations constitute the hypostases" resolution of the circularity objection at q.40 a.4 ("la paternità è lo stesso Padre"). No divergence from Garrigou-Lagrange's synthesis found.
