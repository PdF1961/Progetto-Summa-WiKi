# Il Male come Privazione

Aquinas's metaphysics of evil, from [[sul-male-de-malo]] (*Quaestiones disputatae de malo*), Question 1.

## Evil is not a positive being

Every agent acts for an end/good; goods and agents form a hierarchy topped by a first good/agent (God). Since all things derive from this first cause, and the first cause is the first good, all things deriving from it are, as such, particular goods. Evil has no positive being of its own — nothing tends toward it, nothing desires it, and (since everything desires to exist and shuns what threatens its existence) evil, opposing the good, opposes being itself.

## Evil exists only in the good, as privation

**Evil is the privation of a perfection due by nature.** Privation exists only in a subject that lacks a perfection it ought to have (by nature, by prior possession, or by potency to acquire it) — so privation exists only in a being-in-potency, which as such is itself good. Hence evil can exist only *in* a good (a subject), never as a free-standing thing. This is the technical *privatio boni* doctrine, directly continuous with the potency/act framework of [[Dottrina di Atto e Potenza]]: evil is not a rival positive principle to being (against Manichean dualism) but a deficiency relative to what a thing's nature calls for.

## The good is (accidentally) the cause of evil

Since every agent acts for a good, evil has no cause *per se* — it is never in the agent's intention — only accidental causes, or causation by another evil (a deficiency in the agent). All causality traces back to a first agent, which as such is good. Example: fire acts to impress its own form on things; it is accidental to that action that water loses its form. Morally: moral evil's cause is not the sensible object that attracts the will (which does not compel it) but the will itself, which, letting itself be attracted, turns toward that object against the order of reason and divine law.

## Two kinds of evil: pena and colpa

For the rational creature, evil divides into **male della pena** (evil of punishment/suffering) and **male della colpa** (evil of fault/guilt) — mirroring how good divides into perfection of form/habit and perfection of action. *Pena* resides in the agent (deprived of form, habit, or operative capacity) and is against the will (suffered); *colpa* resides in the action and is according to the will (willed). **There is more evil in *colpa* than in *pena***: a man is called bad for a fault, not a punishment; since God is goodness itself, what is further from God is less good, and fault is further from God than punishment (God is author of punishment, never of fault); God inflicts punishment precisely to avoid fault (as a doctor amputates a hand to save the body), so punishment must be a lesser evil than the fault it prevents; and *colpa* is an act (a defect intrinsically) while *pena* is a suffering (not itself a defect — the sufferer is *on the way to* defect, as the sick man is not dead but on the way to death).
