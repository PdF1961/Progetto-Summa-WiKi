# Il Cardine della Metafisica Tomista: tre interpretazioni a confronto

A cross-source observation surfaced only by reading Garrigou-Lagrange, Gilson, and Mondin side by side: each proposes a different candidate for the single organizing "keystone" (*cardine*) of Aquinas's metaphysics, while claiming fidelity to the same texts.

## Garrigou-Lagrange: the real distinction between potency and act

Per [[essenza-e-attualita-del-tomismo]] and [[la-sintesi-tomistica]]: the whole system radiates from **the real distinction between potency and act**, and within it, between finite essence and the act of existing. Every other Thomistic thesis (the five ways, analogy of being, soul as sole substantial form, natural law) is derived as an application of this one distinction — see [[Dottrina di Atto e Potenza]]. Polemical target: **Suárez**, for treating potency as a degree of "imperfect act" rather than a really distinct capacity, which (Garrigou-Lagrange argues) collapses the demonstrative force of the proofs of God's existence into mere probabilism.

## Gilson: being, hierarchy, and analogy

Per [[le-thomisme-gilson]]: the entire fabric of Thomism is woven from **the idea of being** (*l'être*) itself — from which the convertibility of being/unity/truth/goodness follows, and from which the universe's hierarchical structure of participated being, held together by analogy rather than univocity, is derived — see [[Gerarchia degli Esseri e Analogia]]. Gilson's polemical target is different in kind: not Suárez, but the mistaken reading of Aquinas **as a Neo-Platonist/Plotinian**, which he refutes by insisting Aquinas's hierarchy is "an ordered discontinuity" grounded in Augustine's personal Creator-God, not Plotinus's emanating One.

## Mondin: esse conceived intensively

Per [[mondin-dizionario-tommaso]]: explicitly disagreeing with "many authoritative scholars (Gilson, Masnovo, Maritain, Fabro)" who treat the real essence/esse distinction as *the* cardinal doctrine, Mondin proposes that the true keystone is rather **esse conceived intensively** — as *actualitas omnium actuum*, the actuality underlying and grounding all other acts — with the real distinction remaining indispensable but not itself foundational; it is, on Mondin's view, a consequence of taking esse intensively, not the starting point. See [[Dottrina di Atto e Potenza]] § "Essence as limiting principle."

## Confirmation from the rest of La Sintesi Tomistica (Parti II-VII)

Having read the whole of *La Sintesi Tomistica* (not just Parte Prima and Parte Ottava), Garrigou-Lagrange's claim that essence/esse is the generative root is borne out with remarkable consistency across every subsequent treatise, not just the metaphysical opening: the Trinity's "*tres Personae non habent nisi unum esse*" ([[La Santissima Trinità (Sintesi Tomistica)]]), Christ's "*unum esse in Christo*" ([[Incarnazione, Unione Ipostatica e Mariologia]]), and the obediential-potency doctrine running through angelology, grace, and Mariology alike ([[Angeli e Anima Umana (Sintesi Tomistica)]], [[Teologia Morale nella Sintesi Tomistica]]) are all, explicitly, applications of the *same* essence/esse distinction — and Suárez's denial of that distinction is traced, book-length, as the single point of departure behind his positions on all these otherwise unrelated-seeming questions. This is unusually strong internal evidence for Garrigou-Lagrange's own "one root principle" thesis about his own book, whatever one concludes about Thomism generally.

## Assessment

These are not contradictory claims about *what Aquinas's texts say* — all three authors cite overlapping passages (*Summa contra Gentiles*, *De ente et essentia*, the *Summa Theologiae*'s treatment of God's simplicity) and agree on the object-level doctrine (creatures are composed of essence and a really distinct act of existing; God alone is *ipsum esse subsistens*). They differ in **what each treats as most explanatorily fundamental** — a difference of systematic emphasis and pedagogical entry point, not of doctrine. Interestingly, [[Alasdair MacIntyre]]'s independent, English-language treatment in [[god-philosophy-universities]] ch. 9 lands closest to Garrigou-Lagrange's framing (essence/existence composition as *the* central achievement letting Aquinas "go beyond Aristotle"), without engaging Gilson's or Mondin's alternative framings at all — suggesting the act/potency-centered reading may be the more common default outside specialist Thomist historiography.

## Verified against the primary text (2026-07-26)

*S.Th.* I, q.3, a.4 ("Se in Dio l'essenza e l'essere siano la stessa cosa") — read directly, see [[somma-teologica-de-deo-uno]] — confirms the doctrine all three authors build on: existence differing from essence would require a cause, either from the essence's own principles (impossible) or an external agent (impossible for God as first cause). This is Aquinas's own primary-text statement of the thesis Garrigou-Lagrange, Gilson, and Mondin each take as (or build from) Thomism's keystone.
